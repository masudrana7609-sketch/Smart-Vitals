# Assumptions log

The brief: *"You are free to make reasonable assumptions ... All assumptions must be clearly stated in your report."* Add each one as you make it.

| ID | Assumption | Why it is reasonable | What changes if it is wrong |
|---|---|---|---|
| A1 | Each wearable sends one reading a minute | Typical of consumer wearables, and of our Week 6 simulation | Faster readings mean more messages: check the API keeps up (S4 load test) |
| A2 | Normal / Warning / Critical labels come from a points rule we designed (`src/labelling.py`), applied to the simulator's true vitals | No real patient data is allowed, so labels must be simulated; using the true (not noisy) vitals means the model cannot simply copy the rule | The model may only re-learn our rule: discussed as a limitation |
| A3 | The live demo has 30 simulated patients and 3 clinicians (10 patients each); the model is trained on a separate batch of 80 simulated patients × 3 days (about 346,000 readings) | Enough to show access control and all three risk groups while keeping the demo readable; more patients give the model more varied examples | A real ward or community caseload is larger: check the API and database keep up (load test in S4) |
| A4 | The simulator runs 60× faster than real time by default (one simulated minute per real second); it can be set back to real time | A wearable sends about one reading a minute (A1); 60× lets a 10-minute test (T1) and the live demo run in seconds | Timing-based behaviour (late readings, alert reminders) is measured in simulated time, not wall-clock time |
| A5 | The model is good enough if recall for Critical is at least 0.90 on unseen patients, with no more than 5 false alarms per 100 Normal readings | A missed Critical means no alert (R4), so recall comes first; the cap keeps alarm fatigue in check. The starter forest reached 0.81 on the demo data and 0.62 on our simulator data (notebook 01) | If the target is not met, a rule-based safety net (hard limits) still raises alerts; reported as a limitation |
| A6 | A patient has at most one open alert. It stays open until a clinician acknowledges it, sends a reminder after 15 minutes, and a new alert is raised at once if the reading gets worse | Reduces alarm fatigue without hiding a deterioration | Clinicians who never acknowledge would get repeated reminders; a real system would escalate to another clinician |
| A7 | Readings are kept for 30 days; alerts and the audit log for 365 days, then purged automatically | UK GDPR storage limitation and data minimisation: trends need weeks, not years, of raw readings | A real NHS deployment would follow the NHS Records Management Code of Practice, which may require longer retention |
| A8 | Clinicians are assigned their patients in advance, and every patient has exactly one assigned clinician | Keeps access control (R5) and alert routing (R4) simple and testable | Shared care or shift handover would need team-based access rules |
| A9 | Risk group comes from a points rule we invented (`src/risk.py`): age band, conditions (arrhythmia and COPD-like count double), admissions in the last 12 months, and a past critical event. Low 0–2, Medium 3–5, High ≥ 6 | Risk of deterioration rises with age, multimorbidity and recent hospital use; a transparent rule is easy to explain and to test | A validated tool (e.g. a frailty index) would group some patients differently; the alert logic does not depend on the exact cut-offs |
| A10 | A reading is Critical if its NEWS2-inspired total is 5 or more, or any single parameter scores 3; Warning if the total is 3–4; otherwise Normal | NEWS2 treats 5+ as the key threshold for an urgent response and a single red score as needing urgent review (RCP, 2017); missing a deterioration is worse than a false alarm | Treating a single red score as Warning would cut alerts but miss some genuine emergencies |
| A11 | Glucose (mmol/L) scores: < 3.0 → 3, 3.0–3.9 → 2, 4.0–13.9 → 0, 14.0–19.9 → 2, ≥ 20 → 3. Our own bands: glucose is not part of NEWS2 | Below 4.0 mmol/L is the usual UK hypoglycaemia threshold and below 3.0 is serious; very high glucose risks ketoacidosis | Clinicians may want different cut-offs per patient: R10 thresholds allow this |
| A12 | About half of COPD-like patients have a clinician-set SpO₂ target of 88–92% and are scored on NEWS2 SpO₂ Scale 2 | RCP (2017) reserves Scale 2 for confirmed hypercapnic respiratory failure, decided by a clinician | Scoring these patients on Scale 1 raises constant false alarms: notebook 01 shows exactly this |
| A13 | Deterioration episodes happen 0.4 / 1.0 / 2.0 times per patient per day for Low / Medium / High risk, last about 1.5–10 hours, and are mostly moderate | Chosen so about 6–7% of readings are not Normal, and higher risk means more episodes | Real rates for MedLink's patients are unknown; the model's results depend on how often it sees each episode type |
| A14 | Only five vital signs are measured: pulse, SpO₂, blood pressure, temperature and (for diabetics) glucose. No breathing rate, consciousness or oxygen use | These are what the brief lists and what wrist wearables can measure; wearables estimate breathing rate poorly | Our score is a partial NEWS2 and underestimates some patients: stated as a limitation |
| A15 | Sensor noise and fault rates: missing vital 2%, duplicates 1%, late 1%, impossible values 0.3% of readings; silent devices 0.3 and stuck sensors 0.2 times per patient per day | High enough to test every R9 case many times; low enough that most data is clean | Real failure rates depend on devices and network; the rates are settings and can be raised for stress tests |

| A16 | Blood pressure is scored exactly as NEWS2 does: no points for systolic BP between 111 and 219 mmHg, so a hypertensive crisis below 220 is labelled Normal (decision Q1, 9 Nov 2026) | Stays faithful to the published, validated score instead of inventing a band | Patients with dangerously high but sub-220 BP get no alert; stated as a limitation. A per-patient threshold (R10) could add one |
| A17 | About 25% of simulated patients have diabetes (decision Q2, 9 Nov 2026) | Enough diabetic patients in the unseen test set to check the model works as well for them (ethics checklist: fairness) | Higher than the general adult population; results are not a prevalence estimate |
| A18 | The model uses the median of each vital over the patient's last 5 readings, so the API keeps each patient's last 5 readings in memory or reads them from the database. Readings are ordered by measured time; a late reading joins the window if it arrives in time | A short median smooths sensor noise and single glitches; it cut false alarms by two to three times in notebook 02 | A very late reading cannot change an alert already raised; after a silent spell the window holds older readings |
| A19 | A reading is called Critical when the model's probability of Critical is at least 0.153, a threshold tuned on the training patients to find 92% of Critical readings | A5 puts recall first; tuning on training patients keeps the test honest | Retraining on new data needs a new threshold, saved with the model (`models/model_v2.json`) |
| A20 | While simulating, the feed's clock is used: each message carries its simulated arrival time (`sent_at`), and lateness and reminders are measured in simulated minutes (`CLOCK=feed`). With real devices the server's own clock is used (`CLOCK=wall`) | At 60x speed, server time and simulated time drift apart by a factor of 60 | A device could lie about its times; real devices need trusted time (NTP) and the server clock |
| A21 | An alert is raised after 1-3 consistent readings, not on a single abnormal value, because the model and rule look at the last-5 median | Single readings are noisy; in notebook 02 the median cut false alarms two to three times | About 2 minutes' delay on a sudden collapse; a single extreme reading on its own may not alert |
| A22 | The NEWS2-inspired rule on the last-5 median can also call a reading Critical (safety net) | On unseen patients it raised recall from 0.979 to 0.988 for 0.02 more false alarms per 100 Normal | A rule mistake now also becomes an alert; the alert records which one decided |
| A23 | A heart rate, blood pressure or glucose value repeated exactly 10 times in a row is a stuck sensor and is ignored | Real values vary every minute; SpO2 and temperature repeat naturally, so they are not checked | A genuinely steady patient could be flagged; the value is then treated as missing, not as abnormal |
| A24 | A reading that arrives more than 30 minutes late is stored but raises no new alert | The clinician should act on the patient's current state; newer readings already show it | A long network outage could hide an episode until newer readings arrive |
| A25 | Two roles: clinicians (their own patients only) and an admin (all patients, thresholds, audit log). Each patient has one clinician (A8) | Matches R5 and R10 and keeps access rules testable | Real teams need shared access, cover and handover, and a separate data-protection role |
| A26 | A login lasts 30 minutes and is kept in the browser tab's sessionStorage | Short enough to limit a stolen token's use; the tab closing ends it | Clinicians on a long shift must log in again; a real system would refresh tokens and support single sign-on |
| A27 | Per-patient limits replace the default bands for that vital: inside scores 0, outside is a red score; a model Critical with every vital inside the limits becomes Warning (ADR 7) | Lets a clinician encode a patient's usual values, which the model cannot know | A limit set too wide could hide deterioration in that vital |
| A28 | Repeated views by the same person of the same page and patient are audited once per 5 minutes | The dashboard refreshes every 5 seconds; logging each refresh would bury the meaningful events | Exact view counts are not kept; logins, changes and refusals always are |
